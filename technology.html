1 · Anatomy of a drone

Every UAV is a stack of six subsystems. A venture usually differentiates on one or two and integrates the rest.

Airframe

Structure & aerodynamics — multirotor arms, fixed wing, or VTOL hybrid. Sets weight budget and drag.

Propulsion

Your focus. Energy source + converter + thrust: battery/motor, engine/prop, or hybrid generator set.

Flight controller

The autopilot (e.g. Pixhawk/PX4, ArduPilot, or proprietary) — stabilises and navigates.

Navigation & sensing

GNSS/RTK, IMU, barometer, magnetometer; optionally LiDAR, RGB/multispectral, radar.

Comms & C2

Radio/telemetry link, RC, and increasingly 4G/5G & BVLOS datalinks.

Payload

The revenue-generating part: sensors, spray tank, cargo box, or ISR gimbal.

Strategic note: the flight controller, GNSS, and comms are largely commoditised. Competing there is hard. Propulsion — where you have deep-tech expertise — is where a small team can build a genuine, patentable advantage.

2 · The endurance problem — why chemistry is the wall

Two numbers govern how long any aircraft stays up: how much usable energy it carries per kilogram (specific energy), and how efficiently it converts that energy to thrust. Batteries lose badly on the first number.

Energy carrierRaw specific energyUsable after conversion*Note
Li-ion battery (pack)~150–250 Wh/kg~150–250 Wh/kgWeight is constant — a flat battery weighs the same as a full one.
Li-S / advanced (lab)~350–500 Wh/kg~300–450 Wh/kgEmerging; cycle-life & safety still maturing.
Petrol / gasoline~12,000 Wh/kg~3,000–3,600 Wh/kgSmall 2-stroke ~25–30% efficient; tank empties as you fly.
Diesel / heavy fuel (Jet-A)~11,900 Wh/kg~3,500–4,500 Wh/kgHigher efficiency + single-fuel logistics for defence.
Hydrogen (fuel cell)~33,000 Wh/kg (fuel only)~1,000–2,000 Wh/kg (system)Tank & stack mass dominate; excellent if stored well.

*Usable = raw × conversion efficiency (engine or fuel-cell + drivetrain). Order-of-magnitude figures for intuition, not design values.

The takeaway: even after a small engine throws away ~70% of the fuel's energy as heat, a kilogram of petrol still delivers 10–20× more useful energy than a kilogram of battery. That gap is why fuel wins for long-endurance and heavy-lift — and it is a chemistry gap that better motors cannot close.

The multirotor penalty

Multirotors must spend energy just to hover — there is no wing generating free lift. Hover power scales with (weight)^1.5 / √(rotor area). Adding a heavy battery to fly longer adds weight, which raises hover power, which demands yet more battery — a diminishing-returns spiral. A lightweight, energy-dense fuel source breaks the spiral, which is exactly why hybrid multirotors post 2–4× the endurance of pure-electric ones.

4 · Hybrid architectures in detail

The most promising path is not a bare engine — it's a hybrid that keeps electric-grade control while a fuel powerplant supplies the energy.

Architecture A

Series hybrid (engine → generator → battery → motors)

The engine never touches a propeller. It runs at its most efficient RPM to spin a generator, feeding a DC bus with a small buffer battery. Electric motors do all the flying, so you keep multirotor agility and full-authority flight control. This is the dominant architecture in today's long-endurance hybrid multirotors (Harris Aerial Carrier H6, Skyfront Perimeter, YANGDA YD6, Foxtech, etc.).

Design levers: engine BSFC map, generator efficiency, rectifier/DC-DC losses, buffer-battery sizing for peak transients (gusts, aggressive manoeuvres), thermal management.

Architecture B

Direct-drive IC (engine → propeller)

Classic on fuel-powered fixed-wing UAVs. Lightest and simplest for cruise, unbeatable for long-range mapping/ISR. Not viable for hover-precise multirotor work, so it pairs naturally with fixed-wing or the cruise phase of a VTOL.

Architecture C

Parallel hybrid (engine + motor share the shaft)

Motor provides burst power for VTOL takeoff; engine handles efficient cruise; both can drive the prop. Best power-to-weight for heavy-lift VTOL cargo, but mechanically the most complex — clutch, gearbox, and control-blending are hard engineering problems (and patent opportunities).

Recommended starting point

For a first product, a series-hybrid multirotor or hybrid-VTOL is the pragmatic beachhead: it delivers the endurance story buyers want, reuses mature electric flight control, and puts your differentiation exactly where your expertise is — the engine + generator “range-extender” module. That module can even become a standalone product you sell to other airframe makers.

5 · Key design parameters & formulae

All-Up Weight (AUW / MTOW)
Total flying mass. Sets the DGCA category (Nano/Micro/Small/Medium/Large) and everything downstream.
Payload fraction
Payload ÷ AUW. Battery multirotors ~10–25%; fuel/hybrid systems push higher because energy mass is lower per flight-hour.
Disc loading
Thrust per rotor swept area (N/m²). Lower = more efficient hover, larger props. A core multirotor efficiency knob.
Power loading
Thrust per watt (g/W). Higher is better; drives motor/prop and engine sizing.
BSFC
Brake-Specific Fuel Consumption (g/kWh) — the engine efficiency metric you'll obsess over. Lower = more flight per litre.
Specific power
Powerplant output per kg (kW/kg). The engine's power-to-weight; decisive for lift.
Endurance
≈ usable energy ÷ average power draw. Fuel systems win by carrying far more usable energy per kg.
Cruise vs hover power
Fixed-wing cruise can be 3–5× cheaper than multirotor hover — the reason VTOL hybrids exist.
TSFC / thermal load
Engine waste heat must be managed; airflow, cylinder design, and cooling are integration constraints.
Where your CFD / combustion / thermal background is a weapon: BSFC optimisation, scavenging and port design in small 2-strokes, EFI calibration, cooling-airflow CFD, vibration isolation, and prop–slipstream interaction are all deep, under-served problems in the <10 kW UAV-engine class. This is textbook first-principles engineering territory.

The gap: almost none of these are Indian, and few are price-optimised for Indian agriculture or built around locally serviceable engines and fuel. That's the opening — a cost-engineered, India-manufactured hybrid powertrain.

7 · Where hydrogen fits (your adjacent expertise)

Given a background in hydrogen-energy and safety, hydrogen fuel cells are a natural second horizon. Fuel cells give electric-grade quiet, clean operation with better specific energy than batteries — but today the tank and stack mass, cost, and near-total absence of rural H₂ refuelling make them a later-stage or defence/urban niche play, not a launch product. Keep it on the roadmap as a differentiation option once the combustion-hybrid business is running.

8 · Research gaps & your defensible angle

  • Efficient sub-10 kW UAV engines — most small 2-strokes are hobby-grade and inefficient; EFI + optimised combustion for continuous-duty flight is under-developed.
  • Integrated range-extender module — a compact, vibration-isolated engine-generator-controller “brick” sold as a unit is a product in itself.
  • Thermal & vibration integration — cooling airflow, exhaust routing, and isolating engine vibration from sensitive avionics/cameras is hard and CFD-solvable.
  • Multi-fuel / heavy-fuel small engines — single-fuel (diesel/Jet-A) capability is highly valued by defence and under-served at small scale.
  • India cost-engineering & serviceability — a powertrain built around locally available fuel, parts, and rural repair skills.
  • Hybrid energy management — control logic that blends engine + buffer battery for gust transients and efficiency is patentable software-on-hardware.
Positioning: don't frame the company as “a drone maker” (crowded). Frame it as “the powertrain company for long-endurance drones” — you can sell finished drones and license/sell the propulsion module. That is the defensible, expertise-aligned position.
