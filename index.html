The Mission Gap

Why Endurance Still Matters

Battery-powered UAVs are well suited to many short-duration operations. Missions involving longer flight time, repeated battery changes, heavier payloads or remote deployment can require a different energy architecture. AgniLift is researching how compact engines and hybrid power systems can expand the practical operating envelope of small and medium UAV platforms.

Endurance limitations

Long missions can require repeated battery changes, charging infrastructure and additional field equipment.

Payload trade-offs

Increasing battery capacity can reduce the weight available for useful mission payloads.

Remote operations

Agricultural, industrial and emergency missions may take place far from reliable charging infrastructure.

Our Approach

A Different Approach to UAV Energy

Our research brings together compact propulsion, intelligent power management, airframe integration and modular payload systems.

Compact IC propulsion

Investigation of small, fuel-powered engines designed around UAV weight, vibration and efficiency requirements.

Hybrid power systems

Research into engine-generator architectures that retain precise electric-motor control while increasing available mission energy.

Integrated aircraft design

Propulsion, structure, avionics and payloads engineered as one complete aerial system.

Field-focused development

Design decisions guided by practical agricultural, industrial, logistics and remote-area operating conditions.

Propulsion Architecture

Three Propulsion Paths Under Study

Each architecture suits different mission profiles. We are researching where each is most appropriate rather than treating any one as universally superior.

01

Fuel-powered direct drive

Best suited for

  • Fixed-wing endurance
  • Surveying
  • Mapping
  • Long-range observation
Primary research direction 02

Series hybrid

Best suited for

  • Multirotor endurance
  • Hybrid VTOL
  • Flexible electric propulsion
  • Modular power management
03

Parallel hybrid

Best suited for

  • Peak-power assistance
  • Heavy-payload research
  • Specialized VTOL architectures

Development Path

From Research to Reliable Flight

A staged research and development path. Each stage is stated honestly as an intended direction of work — not as completed or validated.

01 — Feasibility

Define & compare

Mission definition, propulsion comparison, aircraft sizing and safety analysis.

02 — Powerplant testing

Bench evaluation

Engine selection, propeller matching, vibration analysis and thermal evaluation.

03 — Integrated prototype

System build

Airframe, propulsion, avionics, controls and payload-interface development.

04 — Controlled flight testing

Fly & refine

Progressive test flights, data collection, system refinement and reliability assessment.

05 — Application pilots

Field trials

Controlled trials with research, agricultural and industrial collaborators.
