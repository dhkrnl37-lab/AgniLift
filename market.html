India's drone market is small today but growing fast, pulled by defence modernisation, precision agriculture, logistics and infrastructure. One widely-cited estimate puts unit procurement roughly doubling from ~8,400 units (2025) to ~16,800 (2030). Defence & security is projected to be the fastest-growing slice (~26% CAGR), with agriculture close behind on the back of subsidies.

Use figures with care: published market sizes vary widely by definition (civil vs defence, hardware vs services). Treat these as directional. For a pitch, triangulate across ≥2 reports and state your assumptions. Sources on the Resources page.

3 · Why now — the tailwinds

Policy push

Liberalised Drone Rules 2021, a drones-and-components PLI scheme, import restrictions on foreign drones favouring domestic makers, and "Make in India" defence procurement.

Subsidised demand

Namo Drone Didi and agri-drone subsidies are literally paying for drones to enter Indian farms — de-risking the buyer.

Cost of ownership pain

Operators running battery fleets feel the swap/charge downtime and pack-replacement cost — a concrete wedge for an endurance story.

Fuel is already there

Petrol/diesel distribution reaches every Indian village; hydrogen and dense charging do not. Your energy source rides existing infrastructure.

4 · Government schemes to plug into

Namo Drone Didi
Central scheme (~₹1,261 Cr outlay) providing drones to ~14,500 women SHGs for agri rental services, with up to 80% subsidy (capped ~₹8 lakh) plus low-interest loans. A ready channel of subsidised buyers.
Drone PLI
Production-Linked Incentive for drones & components — rewards domestic manufacturing value-add. Relevant if you make powertrains/airframes in India.
Agri-drone subsidies
Various central/state subsidies (SMAM, custom hiring centres, nano-fertiliser push) reduce buyer cost of agri-drones.
Startup India / DPIIT
Recognition unlocks tax benefits, easier compliance, and access to government tenders & funds.
iDEX (Defence)
Innovations for Defence Excellence funds/pilots defence-tech startups — a route into defence UAV programs.
Institutional / IIT support
Incubators, research grants (e.g. ANRF/SERB, DST, MNRE for energy), and IIT lab access — a strong fit given your academic base.
Action: map each scheme to a milestone in the Roadmap. Subsidy-eligibility often requires DGCA type certification and local manufacturing — design for eligibility from day one.

5 · Regulation — the Drone Rules, 2021

  • Governing framework: Drone Rules, 2021 (Ministry of Civil Aviation), administered by DGCA via the Digital Sky platform.
  • Categories by weight: Nano / Micro / Small / Medium / Large (see Drones & Engines).
  • Type Certificate (TC): required before a model is manufactured, imported or operated — except nano & recreational model aircraft. Issued by DGCA (Rule 32) via accredited testing labs against defined design/safety/airworthiness standards.
  • UIN registration: every drone must carry a Unique Identification Number, registered online via Digital Sky.
  • Remote Pilot Certificate: pilots (above nano/micro thresholds) trained & certified via DGCA-approved Remote Pilot Training Organisations (RPTOs).
  • Airspace zones: green / yellow / red zones on the airspace map; red near airports & sensitive sites. BVLOS & beyond-limits ops need special permissions.
  • Import policy: import of foreign drones is restricted (to protect domestic industry) — a tailwind for a Make-in-India venture, but engine/component imports have their own rules.
Not legal advice. Rules evolve. Before design freeze or any commercial operation, confirm the current requirements directly with DGCA / Digital Sky and, ideally, an aviation-regulatory consultant. Certification cost and lead time are real line items — budget for them.

7 · SWOT for an IC-engine drone venture

💪 Strengths

  • Rare, deep engine/combustion/thermal expertise — hard to copy
  • Endurance & payload advantage on target missions
  • Rides existing fuel infrastructure across rural India
  • Academic/IIT base → R&D, talent, grants, credibility

⚠️ Weaknesses

  • Hardware is capital-intensive & slow vs software
  • Engines add noise, vibration, maintenance
  • No airframe/flight-controller track record yet
  • Certification cost & time before first sale

🚀 Opportunities

  • Whitespace: no Indian hybrid-endurance leader
  • Subsidised agri demand + defence indigenisation
  • Sell the powertrain module to other OEMs
  • Heavy-fuel & hydrogen as future differentiation

🌩️ Threats

  • Battery energy density keeps improving
  • Regulatory tightening (noise, emissions, BVLOS)
  • Well-funded incumbents could add hybrid lines
  • Import competition / price pressure

8 · Go-to-market thoughts

  • Beachhead: one mission (agri-spray endurance or long-range survey), one region, a handful of design-partner operators.
  • Wedge product: the hybrid range-extender module — you can sell/retrofit it even before a full aircraft is certified, and license it to OEMs.
  • Proof: a public, verified endurance/payload benchmark vs a comparable electric drone is your single most persuasive asset.
  • Channel: plug into subsidy schemes and DaaS operators rather than selling one drone at a time.
  • Defence in parallel: iDEX pilot for a heavy-fuel variant — high margin, credibility, patient capital.
