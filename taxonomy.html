1 · Drone size classes — India's DGCA categories

The Drone Rules, 2021 classify UAVs by Maximum All-Up Weight (MAUW). Your category determines registration, certification, pilot licensing, and where you can fly — so it's a first-order design constraint, not an afterthought.

Certification is done via DGCA-accredited labs. Plan for this early; it is a gating cost and timeline item. See the Roadmap.

2 · Airframe configurations

🔵 Multirotor (quad / hexa / octo)

Rotors only. Vertical take-off, precise hover, simple to fly. Inefficient (must power hover constantly) → short endurance unless hybridised. Best for: agri-spray, close inspection, short ISR.

✈️ Fixed-wing

A wing does the lifting; very efficient cruise → long range/endurance. Needs runway/launcher and space to land. Can't hover. Best for: large-area mapping, corridor survey, long ISR.

🔶 Hybrid VTOL (fixed-wing + rotors)

Takes off vertically like a multirotor, then transitions to efficient wing-borne cruise. Best of both — the fastest-growing commercial class. More complex control. Best for: long mapping/ISR without a runway.

🚁 Single-rotor / helicopter

One large rotor; efficient hover and high payload, mechanically complex. Common for heavy agri-spray in some markets. Best for: heavy hover-endurance tasks.

5 · Rough sizing rules of thumb

Hover power
Multirotors need very roughly 150–250 W per kg of AUW to hover (better with large, slow props / low disc loading).
Thrust margin
Design for a thrust-to-weight of ≥ 2:1 at MTOW for safe control and gust rejection.
Engine sizing (series hybrid)
Generator continuous output should cover average cruise/hover power; the buffer battery covers peaks. Undersize the engine to average, not peak, load.
Fuel for endurance
Fuel mass ≈ (avg power × hours × BSFC). Better BSFC directly buys endurance or payload — your optimisation target.
Payload fraction goal
Aim to beat a comparable battery multirotor's payload-hours by a clear margin, or the added complexity isn't worth it.

Rules of thumb for early feasibility math only — replace with bench data and detailed analysis before committing a design.
